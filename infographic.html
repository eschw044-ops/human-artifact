When Technology Changes Who We Trust

From the printing press to the internet to generative AI, new technologies have changed how people create, share, and trust information.

Each of these technologies made information easier to access, but each one also created new questions about authority, credibility, and who gets to shape what people believe. Click each section to explore what changed.

Printing Press

Information could be copied and spread much faster than handwritten texts.

What changed: Books and written ideas became easier to reproduce and distribute.

What became easier: More people could access religious, scientific, and political ideas.

New challenge: More information also meant more disagreement about which sources should be trusted.

Who gained power: Writers, printers, and readers had more influence over how ideas spread.

The Internet

Information became almost instant and nearly anyone could publish it.

What changed: Information could travel across the world almost immediately.

What became easier: People could search for information, communicate, and publish their own ideas.

New challenge: Misinformation could spread just as quickly as accurate information.

Who gained power: Everyday users could become creators, publishers, and influencers.

Generative AI

Technology can now help create information, not just store or spread it.

What changed: AI can generate writing, explanations, images, and ideas in seconds.

What became easier: People can organize ideas, learn faster, and get help with difficult tasks.

New challenge: It can be harder to know where information came from or whether it is accurate.

Who gains power: People have powerful tools, but they also have more responsibility to question what they receive.

The bigger question:

As technology makes information easier to create and access, does human judgment become more important instead of less?
